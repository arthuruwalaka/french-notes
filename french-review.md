# French review

Quick reference for what we have covered. TCF Canada, starting from zero.

Pronunciations are **English approximations**. A nasal sound (marked **n**) means you do not fully pronounce the **n** — let it stay in the nose.

## Articles

**The**

| Article | Sounds like | When | Example | Example sounds like |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **le** | luh | masculine singular | le livre | luh leevr |
| **la** | lah | feminine singular | la maison | lah may-**zohn** |
| **l’** | l- (joins the next word) | singular before a vowel or silent h | l’école, l’homme | **lay**-kul, **lum** |
| **les** | lay | plural | les livres | lay leevr |

**A / some**

| Article | Sounds like | When | Example | Example sounds like |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **un** | uhn (nasal) | masculine singular | un chat | uhn shah |
| **une** | ewn | feminine singular | une voiture | ewn vwah-**tewr** |
| **des** | day | plural | des livres | day leevr |

How to hear the difference:

- **le** = luh (short, relaxed)
- **la** = lah (open “ah”)
- **les** = lay (like “lay” in *play*, but shorter)

Always learn the article with the noun: **une voiture**, not just *voiture*.

You cannot write **la école** or **le école**. Write **l’école** (sounds like **lay-kul**).

## Gender

Every noun is masculine or feminine. Objects keep one gender:

- **une voiture** (ewn vwah-tewr) — always feminine
- **un livre** (uhn leevr) — always masculine

When **l’** hides the gender, check **un/une**: **une école** (feminine), **un homme** (masculine).

Adjectives agree:

- un petit garçon — uhn puh-tee gar-sohn
- une petite fille — ewn puh-teet fee

## Être — to be

**être** sounds like **eh-truh**. Use it after another verb: *Je veux être médecin.*

After a subject, conjugate it:

| Subject | Sounds like | Form | Sounds like |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| je | zhuh | **suis** | swee |
| tu | tew | **es** | eh |
| il / elle | eel / ell | **est** | eh |
| nous | noo | **sommes** | sum |
| vous | voo | **êtes** | et |
| ils / elles | eel / ell | **sont** | sohn (nasal) |

**es** and **est** sound the same: **eh**. Spelling shows who the subject is.

Use **être** for identity, description, and location:

- Je suis canadien. — zhuh swee ka-na-dyen
- Elle est française. — ell eh frahn-sez
- Nous sommes à l’école. — noo sum ah lay-kul

## Avoir — to have

**avoir** sounds like **ah-vwahr**. **je + ai** contracts: write **j’ai**, never *je ai*.

| Subject | Sounds like | Form | Sounds like |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| je | zhuh | **ai** → **j’ai** | jay |
| tu | tew | **as** | ah |
| il / elle | eel / ell | **a** | ah |
| nous | noo | **avons** | ah-vohn (nasal) |
| vous | voo | **avez** | ah-vay |
| ils / elles | eel / ell | **ont** | ohn (nasal) |

**as** and **a** sound the same: **ah**.

Use **avoir** for possession and age:

- J’ai un chat. — jay uhn shah
- Elle a vingt ans. — ell ah ven tahn (= She is 20 years old)

## Être or avoir?

| English | French | Sounds like |
| --- | --- | --- |
| I am a student | Je suis étudiant. | zhuh swee ay-tew-dyahn |
| I have a cat | J’ai un chat. | jay uhn shah |
| They are at school | Elles sont à l’école. | ell sohn ah lay-kul |
| They have a house | Ils ont une maison. | eel zohn ewn may-zohn |

## Liaison vs elision

**Liaison** = pronounce a silent final consonant because the next word starts with a vowel.

| Written | Sounds like |
| --- | --- |
| les amis | lay-**z**-ah-mee |
| un homme | uh-**n**-um |
| vous êtes | voo-**z**-et |
| ils ont | eel-**z**-ohn |

No liaison before **et**: *un garçon et une fille* — uhn gar-sohn ay ewn fee.

**Elision** = drop a vowel and write an apostrophe.

| Written | Sounds like |
| --- | --- |
| l’école | lay-kul |
| j’ai | jay |
| l’homme | lum |

**Neither:** *tu es* — tew eh.

## à — to / at / in

**à** sounds like **ah**. It is a preposition, not a verb.

| Written | Sounds like | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **à** | ah | to / at / in |
| **a** | ah | has (from **avoir**) |

They sound the same. The accent is only in writing:

- Elle **a** une voiture. — she **has** a car
- Elle va **à** Paris. — she goes **to** Paris

**à** + article often changes:

| Written | Sounds like | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **à + le → au** | oh | to the (masculine) |
| **à + les → aux** | oh | to the (plural) |
| **à + la → à la** | ah lah | to the (feminine) |
| **à + l’ → à l’** | ah l- | to the (before a vowel) |

Examples:

- Je vais **à** Paris. — zhuh veh ah pa-ree
- Je vais **à la** maison. — zhuh veh ah lah may-zohn
- Je vais **à l’**école. — zhuh veh ah lay-kul
- Nous allons **au** Canada. — noo zah-lohn oh ka-na-dah
- Ils vont **aux** États-Unis. — eel vohn oh zay-tah-zew-nee

## Aller — to go

**aller** sounds like **ah-lay**.

| Subject | Sounds like | Form | Sounds like |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| je | zhuh | **vais** | veh |
| tu | tew | **vas** | vah |
| il / elle | eel / ell | **va** | vah |
| nous | noo | **allons** | ah-lohn (nasal) |
| vous | voo | **allez** | ah-lay |
| ils / elles | eel / ell | **vont** | vohn (nasal) |

- Je vais à l’école. — zhuh veh ah lay-kul
- Elle va à la maison. — ell vah ah lah may-zohn
- Nous allons à Paris. — noo zah-lohn ah pa-ree

## Faire — to do / to make

**faire** sounds like **fehr**. It does **not** mean “to take”; **prendre** means “to take.”

| Subject | Form | Sounds like |
| --- | --- | --- |
| je | **fais** | feh |
| tu | **fais** | feh |
| il / elle | **fait** | feh |
| nous | **faisons** | fuh-zohn |
| vous | **faites** | fet |
| ils / elles | **font** | fohn (nasal) |

- Je fais la vaisselle. — I do / I am doing the dishes.
- Il fait un gâteau. — He makes / is making a cake.
- Il fait beau. — The weather is nice.

French present tense can mean both **I do** and **I am doing**. Context tells you which one:

- **Je fais** = I do / I am doing
- **Je suis en train de faire** = I am in the middle of doing

**faisant** (fuh-zahn) means “doing/making,” but it cannot replace the conjugated verb:

- **Je fais** la vaisselle. — I am doing the dishes.
- Il écoute de la musique **en faisant** la vaisselle. — He listens to music while doing the dishes.
- Not: *Je faisant*

Past participle: **fait**. **J’ai fait** = I did / I have done.

## Prendre — to take / to have

**prendre** sounds like **prahn-druh**.

| Subject | Form | Sounds like |
| --- | --- | --- |
| je | **prends** | prahn |
| tu | **prends** | prahn |
| il / elle | **prend** | prahn |
| nous | **prenons** | pruh-nohn |
| vous | **prenez** | pruh-nay |
| ils / elles | **prennent** | pren |

- Je prends le bus. — I take the bus.
- Je prends un café. — I’ll have a coffee.

Past participle: **pris** (pree). **J’ai pris** = I took / I have taken.

## Venir — to come

**venir** sounds like **vuh-neer**.

| Subject | Form | Sounds like |
| --- | --- | --- |
| je | **viens** | vyehn (nasal) |
| tu | **viens** | vyehn (nasal) |
| il / elle | **vient** | vyehn (nasal) |
| nous | **venons** | vuh-nohn |
| vous | **venez** | vuh-nay |
| ils / elles | **viennent** | vyen |

Commands:

- **Viens !** — Come! (one person, informal)
- **Venez !** — Come! (formal or more than one person)
- Venez ici, s’il vous plaît. — Come here, please.

## Savoir — to know

**savoir** sounds like **sah-vwahr**. Use it for facts, information, and knowing how to do something.

| Subject | Form | Sounds like |
| --- | --- | --- |
| je | **sais** | seh |
| tu | **sais** | seh |
| il / elle | **sait** | seh |
| nous | **savons** | sah-vohn |
| vous | **savez** | sah-vay |
| ils / elles | **savent** | sahv |

- Je sais. — I know.
- Je sais cuisiner. — I know how to cook.
- Tu sais où il habite ? — Do you know where he lives?

**Savoir vs connaître**

- **savoir** = know a fact or know how: *Je sais cuisiner.*
- **connaître** = know or be familiar with a person/place: *Je connais Paris.*

## Negation — ne...pas

Put **ne...pas** around the conjugated verb:

- Je sais. → Je **ne sais pas**. — I do not know.
- Il aime le café. → Il **n’aime pas** le café.

Before a vowel or silent **h**, **ne** becomes **n’**. In casual speech, people often drop **ne** (*Je sais pas*), but keep it in careful speech and formal writing.

## Useful question words and expressions

| French | Sounds like | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **où** | oo | where |
| **ou** | oo | or |
| **ici** | ee-see | here |
| **combien** | kohm-byehn | how much / how many |
| **voilà** | vwah-lah | here/there it is; there you go |
| **d’accord** | dah-kor | okay / agreed |
| **s’il vous plaît** | seel voo pleh | please (formal/plural) |
| **s’il te plaît** | seel tuh pleh | please (informal singular) |

The accent changes the meaning: **où** = where, but **ou** = or.

## Ça va, bien, très bien

- **Ça va ?** (sah vah) — How’s it going? / Are you okay?
- **Ça va.** — I’m okay / It’s going fine.
- **Ça va bien.** (sah vah byehn) — I’m doing well.
- **Très bien.** (treh byehn) — Very well.

**ça** means “it/that,” and **va** means “goes” (from **aller**). The expression literally works like “How’s it going?” in English.

## Impersonal il

**il** normally means “he,” but it can be a placeholder meaning “it,” especially for weather, time, and necessity:

- **Il va pleuvoir.** — It is going to rain.
- **Il fait froid.** — It is cold.
- **Il est trois heures.** — It is three o’clock.
- **Il faut étudier.** — It is necessary to study / You need to study.

In **Il va pleuvoir**, **il** does not refer to a man.

## “Some” — partitive articles

Use these for an unspecified amount:

| Form | Use | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **du** = de + le | masculine singular | du pain |
| **de la** | feminine singular | de la soupe |
| **de l’** | before a vowel/silent h | de l’eau |
| **des** = de + les | plural | des pommes |

After a negative, these normally become **de/d’**:

- J’ai du pain. — I have some bread.
- Je n’ai pas **de** pain. — I do not have any bread.
- Je bois de l’eau. — I drink some water.
- Je ne bois pas **d’**eau. — I do not drink any water.

## Passé composé — everyday completed past

**Passé composé** means “compound past.” Use it for completed past actions. It often translates as either **I did** or **I have done**.

Formation:

**present tense of avoir or être + past participle**

- J’**ai mangé**. — I ate / I have eaten.
- J’**ai fait** la vaisselle. — I did the dishes.
- Elle **est partie**. — She left / She has left.

Most verbs use **avoir**. Some movement verbs and reflexive verbs use **être**. With **être**, the past participle usually agrees with the subject: *Il est parti; elle est partie.*

The **passé simple** expresses a similar completed past but is mainly literary. Recognize forms such as *il mangea* or *je fis* when reading; use the **passé composé** in everyday speech and writing.

## Common corrections

- un voiture → **une voiture** (ewn vwah-tewr)
- la ecole / le ecole → **l’école** (lay-kul)
- sui → **suis** (swee)
- somme → **sommes** (sum)
- avon → **avons** (ah-vohn)
- etes → **êtes** (et)
- heuresuses → **heureuses** (uh-ruz)
- Je suis un chat → **J’ai un chat** (jay uhn shah)
- Elles ont à l’école → **Elles sont à l’école** (ell sohn ah lay-kul)
- **a** = has (Elle **a** une voiture)
- **à** = to / at / in (Elle va **à** Paris)
