# Grammar

## Être or avoir?

| English | French |
| --- | --- |
| I am a student | Je suis étudiant. |
| I have a cat | J’ai un chat. |
| They are at school | Elles sont à l’école. |
| They have a house | Ils ont une maison. |

- **être**: identity, description, and location
- **avoir**: possession and age

## Auxiliary verbs

An **auxiliary** is a helping verb. French uses **avoir** or **être** with a main verb’s past participle to form compound tenses:

- Il **est parti**. — He left.
  - **est** = auxiliary; **parti** = main verb’s past participle
- J’**ai mangé**. — I ate.
  - **ai** = auxiliary; **mangé** = main verb’s past participle

**Être** also forms the passive voice:

- Paul ferme la porte. — Paul closes the door.
- La porte **est fermée** par Paul. — The door is closed by Paul.

## Present actions

French does not need a separate “-ing” tense:

- **Je fais** = I do / I am doing
- **Je mange** = I eat / I am eating

Context tells you which meaning is intended. To emphasize an action happening right now, use:

**être en train de + infinitive**

- Je suis en train de faire la vaisselle. — I am in the middle of doing the dishes.

### en + present participle

Use **en + present participle** for simultaneous actions or a method:

- Il écoute de la musique **en faisant** la vaisselle. — He listens to music while doing the dishes.
- J’apprends **en pratiquant**. — I learn by practising.

## Negation

Put **ne … pas** around the conjugated verb:

- Je sais. → Je **ne sais pas**. — I know. → I don’t know.
- Il aime le café. → Il **n’aime pas** le café. — He likes coffee. → He doesn’t like coffee.

Before a vowel or silent **h**, **ne** becomes **n’**.

Other words replace **pas**. Do not use **pas** with them:

| Pair | Meaning | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **ne … pas** | not | **Je ne vois pas.** — I don’t see. |
| **ne … jamais** | never | **Je n’ai jamais vu ça.** — I’ve never seen that. |
| **ne … plus** | no longer | **Je ne mange plus.** — I don’t eat anymore. |
| **ne … plus jamais** | never again | **On n’invite plus jamais.** — We never invite again. |
| **ne … rien** | nothing | **Je ne vois rien.** — I see nothing. |
| **ne … personne** | no one | **Je ne vois personne.** — I see no one. |

**personne** by itself means “person”; **ne … personne** means “nobody/no one.”

In casual speech, people often drop **ne**:

- **Je sais pas.** — I don’t know.
- **Plus jamais on invite.** — We’re never inviting again.

Keep **ne** in careful speech and formal writing.

## Impersonal il

**il** normally means “he,” but it can be a placeholder meaning “it,” especially for weather, time, and necessity:

- **Il va pleuvoir.** — It is going to rain.
- **Il fait froid.** — It is cold.
- **Il est trois heures.** — It is three o’clock.
- **Il faut étudier.** — It is necessary to study / You need to study.

In *Il va pleuvoir*, **il** does not refer to a man.

Common weather patterns:

- **il fait + condition**: *Il fait froid/chaud/beau.*
- **il y a + weather noun**: *Il y a du vent/soleil.*
- **il + weather verb**: *Il pleut/neige.*

In **il fait froid**, *faire* expresses producing a condition; translate the complete idiom as “it is cold.”

French also uses **il** for a masculine person or thing. Context identifies the meaning:

- Paul arrive. Quand arrive-t-**il** ? — When does **he** arrive?
- Le train arrive. Quand arrive-t-**il** ? — When does **it** arrive?

In an inverted question, the **-t-** is only a pronunciation bridge:

- il arrive → arrive-**t**-il ?
- elle arrive → arrive-**t**-elle ?

## Object pronouns and placement

In a normal statement, an object pronoun goes **before the conjugated verb**:

**subject + object pronoun + verb**

| Pronoun | Meaning | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **me / m’** | me, to me, myself | Il **m’**écoute. |
| **te / t’** | you, to you, yourself | Je **t’**aime. |
| **le / l’** | him or masculine “it” | Je **le** vois. |
| **la / l’** | her or feminine “it” | Je **la** vois. |
| **nous** | us, to us, ourselves | Il **nous** aide. |
| **vous** | you, to you, yourself/yourselves | Je **vous** vois. |
| **les** | them | Je **les** aime. |
| **lui** | to him / to her | Je **lui** parle. |
| **leur** | to them | Je **leur** parle. |

Before a vowel or silent **h**, **me, te, le,** and **la** shorten:

- Je te aime → **Je t’aime.** — I love you.
- Il me écoute → **Il m’écoute.** — He listens to me.
- Je le aime / Je la aime → **Je l’aime.** — I love him/her/it.

The pronoun’s role depends on the verb:

- Il **me** voit. — He sees **me**. (direct)
- Il **me** parle. — He speaks **to me**. (indirect)
- Je **me** lave. — I wash **myself**. (reflexive)

Use stressed pronouns such as **moi/toi** after a preposition or for emphasis:

- avec moi — with me
- pour toi — for you
- avec lui — with him
- avec elle — with her
- **Toi**, je t’aime. — **You**, I love.

As an indirect object, **lui** can mean “to him” or “to her.” After a preposition, **lui** means “him,” while **elle** means “her.”

### Exception: affirmative commands

In a positive command, the pronoun goes **after** the verb with a hyphen:

- Donne-**moi** ça. — Give me that.
- Donne-**lui** le livre. — Give him/her the book.
- Écoute-**moi**. — Listen to me.

In a negative command, it returns before the verb:

- Ne **me** donne pas ça. — Do not give me that.

## Reflexive pronouns

A reflexive verb uses a matching pronoun because the subject does the action to itself. **se** is for **il / elle / on / ils / elles**. It is not “we.”

| Subject | Reflexive | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| je | **me / m’** | **Je me lave.** — I wash myself. |
| tu | **te / t’** | **Tu te laves.** — You wash yourself. |
| il / elle / on | **se / s’** | **Il se lave.** — He washes himself. |
| nous | **nous** | **Nous nous lavons.** — We wash ourselves. |
| vous | **vous** | **Vous vous lavez.** — You wash yourself/yourselves. |
| ils / elles | **se / s’** | **Ils se lavent.** — They wash themselves. |

Before a vowel or silent **h**, **me / te / se → m’ / t’ / s’**:

- **Je m’appelle Arthur.** — My name is Arthur.
- **Il s’appelle Nico.** — His name is Nico.
- **Elle s’habille.** — She gets dressed.
- **Il s’est blessé au pied.** — He hurt his foot.

Common uses:

- Reflexive: **Il se réveille tôt.** — He wakes up early.
- Reciprocal: **Ils se parlent.** — They talk to each other.
- General/passive: **Ça se fait.** — People do that. / That is done.

**se promener** vs **promener**:

- **promener** = to walk someone/something: **Je promène le chien.** — I walk the dog.
- **se promener** = to go for a walk: **Je me promène.** — I go for a walk.
- **Je voulais me promener.** — I wanted to take a walk.
- Without **me**, *je voulais promener* is incomplete (walk what?).

In the passé composé, reflexive verbs use **être**:

- **Elle s’est réveillée.** — She woke up.
- **Leur nom s’est transformé.** — Their name changed.

Affirmative commands put the pronoun after the verb, and **te → toi**:

- **Lève-toi.** — Get up.
- **Lavez-vous.** — Wash yourself/yourselves.
- **Ne te lève pas.** — Don’t get up.

## Subject pronoun on

**on** always uses the **il/elle** verb form. It has two common meanings:

1. Informal **we**:
   - **On y va.** — Let’s go / We’re going.
   - On mange. — We are eating.
2. General **people/one/they/someone**:
   - **On dit que** c’est dangereux. — People say it is dangerous.

In conversation, **on** is often more natural than **nous** for “we,” but **nous** remains common in formal writing.

## Pronoun y

**y** sounds like **ee** and normally replaces a place or **à + a thing/idea**:

- Je vais à Paris. → J’**y** vais. — I am going there.
- Nous sommes à l’école. → Nous **y** sommes. — We are there.
- Je pense à mon examen. → J’**y** pense. — I am thinking about it.
- Je suis allé à Paris deux fois. → **J’y suis allé deux fois.** — I went there twice.

Do not normally use **y** for a person:

- Je pense à Marie. → Je pense **à elle**.

Placement:

- J’**y** vais. — before a conjugated verb
- Je veux **y** aller. — before the infinitive it belongs to
- **Vas-y !** — after a positive command
- N’**y** va pas. — before a negative command

## ce, ça, c’est, and possessives

### ce — this/that before a noun

| Form | Use | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **ce** | masculine before a consonant | ce livre |
| **cet** | masculine before a vowel/silent h | cet homme |
| **cette** | feminine singular | cette voiture |
| **ces** | plural | ces livres |

### ça — this/that/it by itself

- Ça va. — It is going well.
- Je veux ça. — I want that.
- Not: *ça livre*

### c’est — it/this/that is

**ce + est → c’est**

- C’est bon. — It is good.
- C’est une voiture. — It is a car.
- Ce n’est pas moi. — It is not me.

Use **c’est** to identify something; use **il/elle est** to describe an already-known person or thing:

- C’est Marie. — This is Marie.
- Elle est française. — She is French.

### Possessives — my, your, his/her, our, their

Possessive adjectives agree with the **thing owned**, not the owner. **me** is not “my”; **me/m’** means “me” before a verb: *Il **me** voit.* — He sees me.

| Owner | Masculine singular | Feminine singular | Plural |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| my | **mon** | **ma** | **mes** |
| your (informal) | **ton** | **ta** | **tes** |
| his/her/its | **son** | **sa** | **ses** |
| our | **notre** | **notre** | **nos** |
| your (formal/plural) | **votre** | **votre** | **vos** |
| their | **leur** | **leur** | **leurs** |

- **mon livre / ma voiture / mes livres** — my book / my car / my books
- **notre maison / nos maisons** — our house / our houses
- **leur nom / leurs noms** — their name / their names
- **Paul aime sa voiture.** — Paul likes his car.
- **Marie aime sa voiture.** — Marie likes her car.

**notre / votre / leur** stay the same in the singular for masculine and feminine. Only the plural changes: **nos / vos / leurs**.

Before a vowel or silent **h**, use **mon/ton/son** even with a feminine noun:

- **mon amie** — my female friend
- **ton école** — your school
- **son histoire** — his/her story

Although **sa** and **ça** sound the same, **sa** shows possession and **ça** means “that/it.”

## de, du, de la, de l’, and des

| Situation | Rule | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Unspecified “some” | **du** masculine, **de la** feminine, **de l’** singular vowel, **des** plural | du pain; de la musique; de l’eau; des pommes |
| After quantity/most negatives | **de/d’** | beaucoup de pain; pas d’eau |
| Specific “of/from/about the” | de + **le → du**, **la → de la**, **l’ → de l’**, **les → des** | parler du film; de la série; de l’école; des étudiants |
| Before infinitive/proper name | **de/d’** | promettre de venir; venir de Paris |

Important:

- **de l’étudiant** = about the student; **des étudiants** = about the students. **De l’** is singular only.
- **des étudiants** = some students; **parler des étudiants** = talk about the students (**de + les**).
- “Some of the students” = **certains des étudiants**.
- A quantity/container uses **de**: *une bouteille d’eau*.

Other common patterns: **changer de voiture**, **changer d’avis**, **parler de cinéma**.

## qui, que, and dont

| Pronoun | Replaces | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **qui** | subject | l’homme **qui arrive** |
| **que** | direct object (no **de**) | le livre **que j’ai lu** |
| **dont** | **de + noun** | la femme **dont je parle** |

- Je parle **du film**. → Le film **dont** je parle.
- Je parle **des étudiants**. → Les étudiants **dont** je parle.
- J’ai **des étudiants** (“some”). → Les étudiants **que** j’ai, not **dont**.
- **dont** can mean “whose”: *la femme dont le fils est médecin*.
- Never repeat **de** after **dont**.

Separate rule: **ne...que** means “only”: *Il ne mange que du riz.*

## Passé composé — everyday completed past

**Passé composé** means “compound past.” Use it for completed past actions. It often translates as **I did** or **I have done**.

Formation:

**present tense of avoir or être + past participle**

- J’**ai mangé**. — I ate / I have eaten.
- J’**ai fait** la vaisselle. — I did the dishes.
- Elle **est partie**. — She left / She has left.

Most verbs use **avoir**. Some movement verbs and reflexive verbs use **être**.

With **être**, the past participle usually agrees with the subject:

- Il est parti.
- Elle est partie.

## Passé simple — literary past

The **passé simple** expresses a completed past action but is mainly literary:

- Everyday: *Le roi a mangé une pomme.*
- Literary: *Le roi mangea une pomme.*

Recognize it when reading; use the **passé composé** in everyday speech and writing.

## How verb tenses are built

| Form | General meaning | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Present** | Happens now or regularly | *je parle* — I speak/am speaking |
| **Passé composé** | Completed past action | *j’ai parlé* — I spoke/have spoken |
| **Near future** | Going to happen | *je vais parler* — I am going to speak |
| **Imperfect** | Ongoing, repeated, or background past | *je parlais* — I was speaking/used to speak |
| **Conditional** | Would happen; also polite | *je parlerais* — I would speak |
| **Simple future** | Will happen | *je parlerai* — I will speak |
| **Present subjunctive** | Desired, necessary, uncertain, or emotional action after a trigger | *que je parle* — that I speak |

Quick guide: **do/am doing · did · going to do · was doing/used to do · would do · will do · “that someone do” after a trigger**
