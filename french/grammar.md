# Grammar notes

## Articles

### Definite (“the”)

| Article | Use | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **le** | masculine singular | le livre |
| **la** | feminine singular | la maison |
| **l’** | singular before a vowel or silent h | l’école, l’homme |
| **les** | plural | les livres, les écoles |

You cannot say **la école**. It becomes **l’école**. The noun is still feminine: **une école**.

### Indefinite (“a / some”)

| Article | Use | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **un** | masculine singular | un chat |
| **une** | feminine singular | une voiture |
| **des** | plural | des livres |

Always memorize the article with the noun: **une voiture**, not just *voiture*.

## Gender

Every French noun is masculine or feminine. Objects have a fixed gender:

- **une voiture** is always feminine
- **un livre** is always masculine

Clues, not rules:

- Often feminine: **-tion**, **-té**, **-ure**
- Often masculine: **-ment**, **-age**, **-isme**

When **l’** hides the gender, check **un/une** or a dictionary: **école (f.)**.

Adjectives agree:

- un petit garçon
- une petite fille
- un livre intéressant
- une histoire intéressante

## Être — to be

Infinitive: **être** = “to be”. After a subject, use a conjugated form.

| Subject | Form | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| je | **suis** | I am |
| tu | **es** | you are |
| il / elle | **est** | he / she is |
| nous | **sommes** | we are |
| vous | **êtes** | you are (formal or plural) |
| ils / elles | **sont** | they are |

Use **être** for identity, description, and location:

- Je suis canadien.
- Elle est française.
- Nous sommes à l’école.

Use the infinitive after another verb:

- Je veux **être** médecin.

## Avoir — to have

Infinitive: **avoir** = “to have”.

| Subject | Form | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| je | **ai** → **j’ai** | I have |
| tu | **as** | you have |
| il / elle | **a** | he / she has |
| nous | **avons** | we have |
| vous | **avez** | you have |
| ils / elles | **ont** | they have |

**je + ai** must contract: write **j’ai**, never *je ai*.

Use **avoir** for possession and age:

- J’ai un chat.
- Elle a vingt ans. (I am 20 years old = I have 20 years)

## Être vs avoir

- **être**: I am / you are / they are / location
- **avoir**: I have / you have / age

| English | French |
| --- | --- |
| I am a student | Je suis étudiant. |
| I have a cat | J’ai un chat. |
| They are at school | Elles sont à l’école. |
| They have a house | Ils ont une maison. |

## Liaison

A liaison happens when a silent final consonant is pronounced because the next word starts with a vowel or silent **h**.

- les enfants → lay-**z**-enfants
- vous avez → vou-**z**-avez
- un ami → un-**n**-ami
- ils ont → il-**z**-ont

Common sound changes:

- **s / x / z** → **z**
- **d** → **t**
- **n** stays **n**

Usually required after articles and pronouns: **les‿amis**, **ils‿ont**.

Do not make liaison before **et**, or after a singular noun in many cases.

Spelling does not change. Liaison is only pronunciation.
