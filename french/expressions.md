## Questions and location

| French | Sounds like | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **où** | oo | where |
| **ou** | oo | or |
| **ici** | ee-see | here |
| **combien** | kohm-byehn | how much / how many |

The accent changes the meaning: **où** = where, but **ou** = or.

- Où vas-tu ? — Where are you going?
- Tu veux du thé ou du café ? — Do you want tea or coffee?
- Viens ici. — Come here.
- Combien de personnes ? — How many people?

## Ça va, bien, très bien

- **Ça va ?** (sah vah) — How’s it going? / Are you okay?
- **Ça va.** — I’m okay / It’s going fine.
- **Ça va bien.** (sah vah byehn) — I’m doing well.
- **Très bien.** (treh byehn) — Very well.

**ça** means “it/that,” and **va** means “goes” (from **aller**). It works like “How’s it going?” in English.

Natural exchange:

> — Ça va ?  
> — Très bien, merci. Et toi ?

## Pointing, agreement, and politeness

| French | Sounds like | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **voilà** | vwah-lah | here/there it is; there you go |
| **d’accord** | dah-kor | okay / agreed |
| **s’il vous plaît** | seel voo pleh | please (formal/plural) |
| **s’il te plaît** | seel tuh pleh | please (informal singular) |

- Voilà le livre. — Here is the book.
- Voilà, c’est fini. — There, it’s finished.
- D’accord. — Okay.
- Venez ici, s’il vous plaît. — Come here, please.

## Stressed pronouns

- **moi** = me/myself
- **toi** = you/yourself (informal singular)

Use them after prepositions, for emphasis, or in short answers:

- C’est pour toi. — It is for you.
- Toi et moi. — You and me.
- Donne-le-moi. — Give it to me.

**vous** means formal singular “you” or plural “you”:

- Vous êtes là. — You are there.
- Je vous vois. — I see you.
