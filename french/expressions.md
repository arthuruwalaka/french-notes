# Useful expressions

## Questions and location

| French | Sounds like | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **où** | oo | where |
| **ou** | oo | or |
| **ici** | ee-see | here |
| **combien** | kohm-byehn | how much / how many |
| **comment** | koh-mahn | how |
| **quand** | kahn | when |
| **qui** | kee | who |
| **que / qu’** | kuh / k- | what / that |

The accent changes the meaning: **où** = where, but **ou** = or.

- Où vas-tu ? — Where are you going?
- Tu veux du thé ou du café ? — Do you want tea or coffee?
- Viens ici. — Come here.
- Combien de personnes ? — How many people?
- Comment faire ? — How do I/we do it? / What should I/we do?
- Quand arrive-t-il ? — When does he/it arrive?
- Qui est-ce ? — Who is that?
- Qu’est-ce que c’est ? — What is that?

For “Who is that?”, **Qui est-ce ?** is more natural than *Qui est ça ?*. For a thing, ask **Qu’est-ce que c’est ?**

## Si — if / yes, contrary to a negative

**si** sounds like **see**.

Use it for a condition:

- Si tu veux, je peux venir. — If you want, I can come.
- **si + il → s’il**: *S’il pleut...* — If it rains…

Use **si**, not **oui**, to contradict a negative statement or question:

> — Tu ne veux pas de café ?
>
> — Si, j’en veux.
>
> — You don’t want coffee?
>
> — Yes, I do.

## Ça va, bien, très bien

- **Ça va ?** (sah vah) — How’s it going? / Are you okay?
- **Ça va.** — I’m okay / It’s going fine.
- **Ça va bien.** (sah vah byehn) — I’m doing well.
- **Très bien.** (treh byehn) — Very well.

**ça** means “it/that,” and **va** means “goes” (from **aller**). It works like “How’s it going?” in English.

Natural exchange:

> — Ça va ?  
> — Très bien, merci. Et toi ?

## Pointing, agreement, and politeness

| French | Sounds like | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **voilà** | vwah-lah | here/there it is; there you go |
| **d’accord** | dah-kor | okay / agreed |
| **s’il vous plaît** | seel voo pleh | please (formal/plural) |
| **s’il te plaît** | seel tuh pleh | please (informal singular) |

- Voilà le livre. — Here is the book.
- Voilà, c’est fini. — There, it’s finished.
- D’accord. — Okay.
- Venez ici, s’il vous plaît. — Come here, please.

## Stressed pronouns

- **moi** = me/myself
- **toi** = you/yourself (informal singular)

Use them after prepositions, for emphasis, or in short answers:

- C’est pour toi. — It is for you.
- Toi et moi. — You and me.
- Donne-le-moi. — Give it to me.

**vous** means formal singular “you” or plural “you”:

- Vous êtes là. — You are there.
- Je vous vois. — I see you.

## Hunger and time

French uses **avoir** (“to have”) for hunger:

- **J’ai faim.** — I am hungry. (literally: I have hunger)
- Tu as faim ? — Are you hungry?

French uses impersonal **il est** for clock time:

- **Il est une heure.** — It is one o’clock.
- **Il est deux heures.** — It is two o’clock.

Use singular **heure** after one and plural **heures** after other numbers.

## Il y a — there is/are or ago

**Il y a** sounds roughly like **eel-yah**.

Before a noun, it means “there is/are”:

- Il y a un livre. — There is a book.
- Il y a deux voitures. — There are two cars.

Before a duration, it means “ago”:

- Je l’ai vu **il y a trois heures**. — I saw him/it three hours ago.
- Il y a deux jours. — Two days ago.

Time comparison:

- **Il est 3 h.** — It is 3 o’clock.
- **Il y a 3 h.** — Three hours ago.
- **Dans 3 h.** — In three hours.
- **Pendant 3 h.** — For three hours.

## Temps — time, weather, or tense

Context determines the meaning of **temps**:

- Je n’ai pas le **temps**. — I do not have time.
- Quel **temps** fait-il ? — What is the weather like?
- Le verbe change de **temps**. — The verb changes tense.

## Names and introductions

- Quel est ton **nom** ? — What is your name/surname?
- Quel est ton **prénom** ? — What is your first/given name?
- Je **m’appelle** Arthur. — My name is Arthur.
- Merci, **monsieur**. — Thank you, sir.
